## Sound Familiar?

When you signed your commercial cleaning contract 90 days ago, everything seemed perfect:
- The owner or senior sales rep gave a polished presentation.
- The first two weeks of cleaning were pristine.
- Restrooms smelled clean, floors sparkled, and trash was emptied without fail.

Fast forward to Month 3:
- Restroom countertops are sticky.
- Trash in executive offices is missed twice a week.
- High dusting on vents and light fixtures hasn't been touched in a month.
- The owner who sold you the contract is nowhere to be found.

In commercial facility management, this phenomenon is so common it has a name: **The Janitorial Honeymoon Effect**.

---

## Why the "Honeymoon Effect" Happens: The Industry's Dirty Secret

Property managers often assume their cleaning company simply "got lazy." But the Honeymoon Effect is actually the predictable result of how traditional janitorial companies structure their business model.

```
[Month 1: Owner / Senior Crew Cleans Building] ──> [Client Signs Contract & Passes Initial Trial]
                                                                  │
                                                                  ▼
[Month 2: Job Re-Assigned to Low-Wage Crew] ──> [Owner Moves to Selling Next Client]
                                                                  │
                                                                  ▼
[Month 3: Zero On-Site Supervisor Audits] ──> [Quality Drops to Lowest Effort Level]
```

### The 3 Structural Causes of Quality Decay:

1. **Bait-and-Switch Crew Assignment**: During Month 1, traditional cleaning companies send their best senior cleaners or business partners to ensure you pass the initial trial period. Once the contract is secured, they re-assign the building to low-wage cleaners who were never properly trained on your building's specific Scope of Work (SOW).
2. **Franchise Margin Squeeze**: Many national cleaning franchises sell contracts to sub-franchisees for a hefty commission. The actual cleaner on-site receives only 40% to 50% of the total contract value. With tight margins, the cleaner is forced to rush through your building in 45 minutes instead of 2 hours.
3. **The Self-Inspection Paradox**: In traditional janitorial setups, the cleaner is responsible for inspecting their own work. They sign paper log sheets on the restroom wall asserting that everything was cleaned. Without an independent third party checking their work at night, quality naturally degrades to the minimum acceptable threshold.
